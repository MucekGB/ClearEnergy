Simple energy-saving tips

Six ways to use less energy at home.

Lights off

Turn off the lights when you leave a room.

Switch off

Turn off unused devices instead of leaving them on standby, when safe.

Choose LED

Choose an LED when you need a new light bulb.

Full loads

Wash a full load of clothes, but do not overload the machine.

Set your heating

Use a timer and thermostat to avoid heating more than you need. Keep your home warm enough.

Compare costs

Check the power on a device label. Use the calculator to estimate its cost.

Renewable energy

Electricity from the sun, wind and water.

Solar

Solar panels turn sunlight into electricity.

Advantage
Makes electricity without burning fuel.
Limitation
Needs daylight. Makes less electricity on cloudy days.

Wind

Wind turns turbine blades to make electricity.

Advantage
Makes electricity without burning fuel.
Limitation
Needs enough wind and a suitable location.

Hydropower

Moving water turns turbines to make electricity.

Advantage
Some plants store water to use later.
Limitation
Dams can affect rivers and wildlife.

How the calculator works

First, we calculate energy use from power and time. Then we multiply energy use by the electricity price.

Formula and units

W
Watts: the power of a device. 1,000 W = 1 kW.
kWh
Energy used by a 1 kW device running for 1 hour.
p/kWh
Price in pence for 1 kWh. 100 pence = £1.

Daily figures are for one day of use. Yearly cost = monthly cost × 12, with the same use each month.

About ClearEnergy

ClearEnergy is a student project for households and students. Our aim is to help people use less energy and understand electricity costs. This energy education supports SDG 7: Affordable and Clean Energy.
